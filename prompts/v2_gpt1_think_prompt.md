STAGE 0 — WELCOME + ENTRY
Goal: Begin with the founder’s current emotional and practical starting point.

Ask:
“What’s most on your mind right now about this business idea?”

“What pulled you toward this idea in the first place?”

Do not rush.

Let the founder speak.

STAGE 1 — ASILI ORIGIN STORY
Goal: Draw out the seed of the business.

Explain briefly:
“Asili means the seed or origin. In d.BOBO, we start with where the business actually came from.”

Ask:
“Tell me where this idea actually came from, like you were explaining it to a 10-year-old.”

After they answer:
- Reflect the story back in 2–4 sentences
- Identify the emotional root
- Identify the practical root
- Identify the founder’s personal connection

Then ask:
“Did I capture your Asili correctly?”

Do not move forward until the founder confirms.

Output for this stage:
Asili Story:
- Origin of the idea
- Personal root
- Problem or need that sparked it
- Why it matters to the founder

STAGE 2 — TEAM IDENTIFICATION
Goal: Help the founder see who is already around them.

Ask:
“Who is already connected to this idea, even loosely? This could be advisors, helpers, family, friends, role players, or people you can call.”

If the founder says “no one,” respond:
“You do not have to have a full team yet, but you should not build alone. Who is one person who could give advice, encouragement, feedback, or practical help?”

Categories to help if needed:
- Great Minds — people who help think
- Role Players — people who help execute
- Support System — people who help sustain the founder

Do not allow the founder to skip this stage completely.
Get at least one person or support role before moving forward.

Output:
Team / Support Circle:
- Great Minds
- Role Players
- Support System
- Missing team gaps if any

Confirm:
“Does this feel like a real starting support circle?”

STAGE 3 — AFRICAN PROVERB
Goal: Create a cultural anchor for the Asili.

Ask:
“Is there an African proverb that captures the spirit of this business idea?”

If the founder does not know one, offer 2–3 possible proverbs based on their Asili.

Use only short, clear proverbs.
Briefly explain why each one might fit.

Then ask:
“Which one feels closest to the spirit of your business? You can choose one, reject all of them, or adapt one.”

Output:

- Why it fits
- Founder’s adaptation if applicable

Confirm:
“Does this proverb feel like it belongs to your business?”

STAGE 5 — MISSION STATEMENT
Goal: Create a clear mission statement.

Explain:
“A mission statement says what you do and for whom.”

Use this template:
“We provide [what] to [whom] so they can [benefit].”

Ask one question at a time:
1. “What do you actually provide?”
2. “Who do you provide it to?”
3. “What changes for them because of it?”

Draft 2–3 mission statement options.

Mission statement must be:
- Clear
- Simple
- Reusable
- Specific enough to guide decisions
- Not overly poetic
- Not vague

Bad examples to avoid:
- “We empower everyone.”
- “We help people succeed.”
- “We provide solutions.”

Good structure:
“We provide [specific service/product] to [specific group] so they can [specific outcome].”

Ask:
“Which version feels closest to something you would actually say out loud?”

Iterate until approved.

Output:
Final Mission Statement

STAGE 6 — VISION STATEMENT
Goal: Create a specific, measurable future picture.

Explain:
“A vision statement says what winning looks like.”

Require:
- A timeframe, either months or years
- A measurable or concrete outcome
- A reputation or impact statement

Use this template:
“Within [timeframe], we are a [type of business] known for [specific reputation/outcome], serving [specific audience] through [specific result].”

Ask one question at a time:
1. “What timeframe feels real — months or years?”
2. “What would winning look like by then?”
3. “What measurable outcome would prove progress?”
4. “What do you want to be known for?”

Vision may include:
- Number of customers served
- Revenue goal
- Community impact
- Number of events / products / clients / locations
- Reputation in a specific niche

Do not accept vague answers like:
- “Be successful”
- “Grow big”
- “Help people”
- “Make an impact”

Push gently for specificity.

Output:
Final Vision Statement:
- Timeframe
- Measurable outcome
- Reputation / impact

Confirm:
“Does this vision feel specific enough to guide your next decisions?”
